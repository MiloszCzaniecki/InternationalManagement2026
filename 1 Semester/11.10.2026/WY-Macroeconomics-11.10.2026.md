Economics lecture covering supply and demand equilibrium, interest rates and inflation after COVID, classical vs Keynesian approaches, economic cycles, and the GDP definition, with several topics flagged for the final exam.

Supply, Demand and Equilibrium
- Demand curve: higher price means fewer units sold; supply curve: higher price or margin means more willingness to produce
- Equilibrium is where buyer and seller meet, illustrated by selling a used car (seller wants 30,000, buyer 20,000, settle at 25)
- The graph will return in later classes, e.g. when discussing minimum wage

Macroeconomics, Interest Rates and Markets
- Macroeconomics treats the economy as an interconnected system, e.g. a distant conflict raising fuel prices, inflation and mortgage rates in Poland
- After COVID, rates were cut from 2.5 to 0.25, money supply rose, supply chains were limited, and inflation hit almost 19 to 20%
- Cheap money fueled a stock bull run; later Fed rate hikes pulled money out and markets fell
- Fed decisions are followed by other central banks and move markets like Bitcoin within minutes

Classical vs Keynesian Approaches (exam topic)
- Adam Smith's classical approach: minimal government, invisible hand lets the market self-regulate
- Competition benefits consumers, e.g. phones improving from 2 megapixels to photographing the moon
- Keynes' interventionism aims to prevent overheating and deep bottoms of the cycle

Economic Cycles (exam topic)
- Four phases: expansion, peak, recession, bottom; GDP, unemployment, wages and consumption shift in each
- 2008 crisis linked to 'NINJA' mortgages (no income, no job, no assets) given during the boom
- Tools: raise rates or taxes to cool a peak; cut rates and taxes, print money, raise benefits at the bottom
- Noted stimulus raises inflation; lecturer agreed there is often no good choice, especially before elections

GDP Definition (exam topic)
- GDP (PKB in Poland): market value of all final goods and services produced in a country in a period, usually one year
- Uses final consumer price, counts only new items, excludes illegal activity, to avoid double counting
- Foreign firms in Poland (LG, Volkswagen, DHL) raise Polish GDP though profits may go abroad
- Usually calculated via spending, with four components; to be covered after the break
