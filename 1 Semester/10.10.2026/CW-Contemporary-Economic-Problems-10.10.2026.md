First session of the Contemporary Economic Problems course: 
-Professor explained course aims, structure, and grading (60% activity, 40% group presentation), then launched the Survive the Century simulation game.

Course Organization
- 13 workshop hours over 4 meetings; Professor delivers all materials and grades all work, coordinator need not be contacted
- Professor is external (University of Wrocław) and may check WSB email only 1 to 3 times per week
- Aim is analytical and systems thinking, not memorizing theory; knowledge itself is not assessed
- Process model: what happens, causes, actors involved or affected, multidimensional consequences, trade-offs, responses
- Example: Polish maximum gasoline price lowers budget income, shifting costs to taxpayers and next generations

Course Content
- Topics: intro to economics vs economy, process approach with case studies, risk types (black swans, grey rhinos, Risk Zoo)
- Later focus on corporate and governmental risk management; final meeting is an application lab with presentations

Assessment
- No exam: activity is 60% (includes attendance), group presentation is 40%, no essay this first semester
- Absences can be offset with compensation assignments, relevant for students aiming at 4.5 or 5
- Presentations: 20 to 25 minutes per group plus 5 to 10 minutes Q&A, at last online meeting end of January
- Must be analytical with a research question, reliable sources, references; every member must speak
- Topics on Moodle are thematic areas to refine; other topics allowed after consulting Professor by email

AI and Submission Rules
- AI tools allowed but outputs must be checked critically; no plagiarism
- Presentations uploaded to a Moodle activity before the 30th of January; contact Professor early about any difficulties

Survive the Century Game
- Students act as editors of a powerful magazine, deciding decade by decade to 2100 with social, climate, economic scores
- Given about 30 minutes from 1:15 PM, individually or in small teams, then discuss results
- Reflect on hard decisions, surprises, and trade-offs or conflicts of interest
