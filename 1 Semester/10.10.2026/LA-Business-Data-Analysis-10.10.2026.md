Professor explained the semester project rules, grading, and class logistics for Business Data Analysis, then began a discussion on what business is and how it connects to data.

Project Groups and Grading
- Groups can have 1 to 4 people; do not cover for members who don't deliver, as each will be questioned on their contribution.
- 50% of grade is the final report and presentation; 50% is the quality of questions and interaction when other groups present.
- Meaningful questions expected, not generic praise
- Presentation style is up to the group, but all members should stand up and take questions; presentation skills are also assessed.

Report, Presentation and Interview Requirements
- Printed copy of the report is required; report is 15 to 20 pages with sources listed; presentation is 2, max 3 summary slides.
- At least one real interview (10+ minutes, no yes/no questions) with questions, transcription and recording attached, ideally before 6 December.
- Share via Google Drive or private email, never upload to Moodle
- Three company options: Kłodzka Café, a one-person QA business, ornconsulting agency; changes must be discussed first.
- AI use is encouraged but students must author the text; recommendations should be pragmatic, non-generic, in plain English.

Scheduling and Class Logistics
- Project steps are sequential, so work must be planned; earlier delivery is encouraged ahead of deadlines.
- One absence allowed with no consequences; each further absence requires an email with a 2-sentence class summary and a non-generic opinion.
- Next onsite class is tomorrow, not in 2 weeks as student first thought.

Discussion: What Is Business?
- Textbook definition (point A to point B, profit) was seen as too abstract; key idea added was interaction, which happens constantly.
- Every interaction with businesses (phone alarm, internet provider, university) generates data, largely uncontrolled.
- CSR discussed:companies only act responsibly when required, and publicize CSR mainly during crises.
